About

I am a Ph.D. student in Computer Science at the Kahlert School of Computing, University of Utah, advised by Prof. Aditya Bhaskara.

My research is on machine learning and algorithms for wireless networks. Radio spectrum is limited, and more and more users have to share it, including sensitive receivers such as radio telescopes that must not be disturbed. I build methods that make this sharing efficient, reliable, and secure: physics-informed models that locate transmitters from sparse measurements, reinforcement-learning schedulers for Open RAN cellular networks, and optimization algorithms with provable guarantees that admit more users without breaking protection limits.

I like working across theory and systems. I prove guarantees for my algorithms and also test them on real data from the POWDER wireless testbed. My work is supported by the U.S. National Science Foundation through the POWDER-ENS and National Radio Dynamic Zones (NRDZ) programs.

Before Utah, I was a full-time Lecturer in Computer Science and Engineering at United International University, Dhaka. I received my B.Sc. in Computer Science and Engineering from the Islamic University of Technology (IUT).

Research

Ongoing

Spectrum sharing with provable protection

Deciding which transmitters can share spectrum near a protected receiver, such as a radio observatory, when the only feedback is whether its interference limit was met. I design local-search algorithms with guarantees on both utilization and protection, motivated by NSF's National Radio Dynamic Zones.

With Aditya Bhaskara and Sneha Kumar Kasera · Under review at SIGMETRICS 2027
Ongoing

Reinforcement learning for Open RAN scheduling

RL schedulers that group users for MU-MIMO so several users share the same radio resources, protect underserved users, deliver packets on time, and stay robust to stale channel reports. I built a Sionna-based multi-cell 5G simulator for this, with training planned on the POWDER testbed.

Code on GitHub · With the Flux Research Group
ACM CoNEXT 2025

Physics-informed transmitter localization

PhARMNet combines terrain-aware propagation modeling with neural RSS prediction to generate realistic synthetic measurements, improving localization where field data is sparse. I led the project from design to publication and released the augmented dataset.

Paper · Dataset
ITCS 2027

Optimizing propagation in probabilistic networks

Adding a few edges to a network so that information reaches every node with high probability. I designed the ball-packing algorithm behind our main approximation result; an independent group has since used it as a benchmark.

arXiv · With Blair D. Sullivan's group
